        The notes

        Read this before you buy.

        Four short notes. All of them are free, and all of them matter more than anything in your bag.

        • Note 01 · Fuel

          Protein, without the overthinking

          • Build each meal around a protein: eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, chicken, fish, tofu or beans.
          • About 25 to 30 grams a meal is a useful target for a lot of active women. A palm-sized portion of meat or fish gets you most of the way there.
          • Shakes and bars fill gaps on busy days. They don't replace meals.
          • Creatine: 3 to 5 grams a day, at any time of day. It's one of the most researched supplements for strength. If you're pregnant or nursing, ask your doctor first.
          See the fuel picks

        • Note 02 · Hydrate

          Drink before you're thirsty

          • Keep a bottle you actually like within reach. You'll drink more of it.
          • Sip through the day, and take a few big gulps every 15 to 20 minutes while you train.
          • Water covers most workouts. For long, hot or very sweaty sessions, an electrolyte mix helps replace the salt you sweat out.
          • Check the color. Pale yellow means you're good. Dark yellow means drink up.
          See the hydration picks

        • Note 03 · Strength

          Lift heavier than you think

          • Two to four strength sessions a week is plenty. Give a muscle group a day off before you work it hard again.
          • Build around a few big moves: squats, hip thrusts or bridges, rows, presses and carries.
          • When the last two reps of a set feel easy, add a rep or a little weight. That's progressive overload, and it's how you keep getting stronger.
          • Lifting won't make you bulky by accident. It makes you stronger, steadier and harder to knock over.
          See the strength picks

        • Note 04 · Restore

          Rest is part of the program

          • Aim for 7 to 9 hours of sleep. That's when your muscles actually rebuild.
          • Roll tight spots slowly for 30 to 60 seconds. It should feel like pressure, not pain.
          • On rest days, walk, stretch or do an easy flow. Gentle movement helps you feel less stiff.
          • Sore for a day or two is normal. Sharp pain, swelling or pain that sticks around isn't, so get it checked.
          See the restore picks

        General tips, not medical advice. If you're pregnant, postpartum, managing a health condition or coming back from an injury, talk to your doctor or a physical therapist before you change your routine.

        Questions

        Good questions.

        Will lifting make me bulky?

        No. Building noticeable muscle takes years of focused training and eating for it. For most women, lifting means looking more defined, feeling stronger and finding everyday things easier.

        Is creatine okay for women?

        For healthy adults, creatine monohydrate is one of the most researched supplements there is, and 3 to 5 grams a day is the usual amount. Some people hold a little extra water at first, which can show up on the scale. If you're pregnant, nursing or managing a kidney condition, ask your doctor first.

        Do I need protein powder?

        No. It's food in a faster form. It helps on busy days, when you travel or when you're not hungry after a workout, but real meals come first.
